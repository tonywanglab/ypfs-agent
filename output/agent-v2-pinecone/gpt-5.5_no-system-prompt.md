## Emergency broad-based bank liquidity program: options for supervisor

### Bottom line

Given a run at a globally significant domestic bank and signs of contagion, the central bank should consider announcing a **broad-based, temporary term liquidity facility for all eligible banks**, explicitly designed to supplement—not replace—the standing facilities. The strongest baseline option is a **Term Auction Facility–style program** because it can provide term funding while reducing discount-window stigma. If the run intensifies, the central bank should be ready to switch to or add a **fixed-rate, full-allotment term facility**.

A useful modern rule is: **in the acute panic phase, lend early, freely, and broadly against sound collateral, with pricing and disclosure designed to minimize stigma.**

---

## Recommended package

### 1. Immediate baseline: Broad-Based Term Auction Facility

**Purpose:** Provide term liquidity to the banking system and reduce stigma associated with use of standing facilities.

**Core design**
- **Eligible borrowers:** All supervised banks with access to standing facilities; consider including material branches/subsidiaries of foreign banks if important to domestic funding markets.
- **Collateral:** All collateral eligible at existing standing facilities, with authority to expand to high-quality but less-liquid assets if needed.
- **Maturities:** Start with 28-day and 84-day loans; add 3-month and 6-month terms if term funding markets remain impaired.
- **Pricing:** Uniform-price auction with a minimum bid rate modestly above the policy rate or relevant risk-free benchmark, but below stressed market term funding rates.
- **Auction size:** Announce large initial allotments; increase quickly if oversubscribed. Avoid being perceived as under-sized.
- **Individual limits:** Cap individual awards, e.g., no more than 10–20% of an auction, to prevent one institution from monopolizing the facility and to signal broad use.
- **Settlement:** Same-day or next-day if urgency is extreme; otherwise short delayed settlement can reduce perception that only desperate banks are participating.
- **Disclosure:** Publish aggregate auction results—total bids, total allotted, stop-out rate, number of bidders—but do not disclose borrower-level use during the acute phase.

**Advantages**
- Reduces stigma relative to the discount window because many banks bid together.
- Provides market information through auction demand and pricing.
- Allows the central bank to control total liquidity injected.
- Strong precedent: the Federal Reserve’s Term Auction Facility was specifically designed to overcome discount-window stigma during the GFC.

**Risks**
- Auctions may not provide immediate liquidity to a bank experiencing same-day outflows.
- If allotments are too small, failed bids can worsen market anxiety.
- If the rate is too high, the program may be stigmatized and underused.

**Best use:** Default first option if the system is under stress but market functioning has not completely collapsed.

---

### 2. Crisis escalation option: Fixed-rate, full-allotment term lending

**Purpose:** Send a strong “liquidity will be available” signal if the run is spreading rapidly.

**Core design**
- **Eligible borrowers:** Same as above.
- **Maturities:** 1-month and 3-month initially; add 6-month or 12-month if term markets are closed.
- **Pricing:** Fixed rate, e.g., policy rate plus a modest spread. In severe stress, avoid punitive pricing.
- **Size:** Full allotment against eligible collateral—banks receive all amounts requested, subject to collateral and risk controls.
- **Collateral:** Broad collateral set with conservative haircuts.

**Advantages**
- Strongest announcement effect.
- Reduces rollover risk immediately.
- Avoids uncertainty about auction allocations.
- Precedent: the ECB’s term refinancing operations moved to fixed-rate full allotment after Lehman; the Fed effectively moved TAF toward full allotment in the most acute phase.

**Risks**
- Greater balance sheet expansion.
- Greater moral hazard if pricing is too cheap or eligibility too loose.
- Could be read as a signal that the crisis is severe.
- Requires robust collateral valuation and operational capacity.

**Best use:** If outflows are accelerating, interbank markets are frozen, or auction allotments are insufficient.

---

### 3. Enhanced standing-facility overlay

**Purpose:** Make existing facilities more usable immediately while the new program is launched.

**Core design**
- Temporarily reduce the standing facility penalty spread.
- Extend maximum maturity from overnight to 30 or 90 days.
- Broaden eligible collateral.
- Increase operating hours and pre-positioning capacity.
- Encourage banks to test operational access.

**Advantages**
- Fastest to implement because it uses existing legal and operational infrastructure.
- Provides immediate liquidity for banks with urgent same-day needs.
- Complements auctions, which may operate weekly or on scheduled dates.

**Risks**
- Standing facilities often carry stigma.
- If used by only one or a few banks, markets may infer weakness.
- May not be enough if collateral eligibility remains too narrow.

**Best use:** Immediate bridge measure, not the main anti-contagion tool.

---

### 4. Extended-collateral term facility

**Purpose:** Address a situation where banks have liquidity needs but insufficient standard collateral.

**Core design**
- Accept wider collateral: performing loan portfolios, high-quality ABS/MBS, covered bonds, corporate credit, and other assets judged sound before the panic.
- Apply conservative haircuts and valuation rules.
- Consider collateral tiers with different rates/haircuts.
- Offer term funding through auction or fixed-rate allotment.

**Advantages**
- Directly addresses collateral scarcity.
- Reduces forced asset sales and fire-sale dynamics.
- Precedents include Bank of England extended-collateral repo facilities and Canadian facilities that added nonstandard collateral.

**Risks**
- More difficult valuation.
- Greater credit risk to the central bank.
- Operationally complex if collateral has not been pre-positioned.
- Too-broad collateral can create political and moral hazard concerns.

**Best use:** If many banks cannot access enough funding using standard collateral.

---

### 5. Contingent backstop announcement

**Purpose:** Strengthen confidence by announcing that additional facilities are ready if needed.

**Core design**
- Announce the baseline term facility now.
- State that the central bank is prepared to increase auction sizes, add maturities, broaden collateral, or move to full allotment if market conditions warrant.
- Pre-clear legal, operational, and accounting steps for escalation.

**Advantages**
- Creates a positive announcement effect.
- Preserves flexibility.
- Reduces need for repeated emergency improvisation.
- Helps markets understand the central bank’s reaction function.

**Risks**
- If too vague, markets may doubt commitment.
- If too specific, it may invite gaming or signal excessive concern.

**Best use:** As part of the communications strategy accompanying Options 1–4.

## Key design choices

### Eligibility

For a broad-based banking facility, eligibility should be rule-based and transparent:
- all solvent, supervised domestic banks;
- possibly domestic branches or subsidiaries of foreign banks if they are important to local funding markets;
- no facility designed solely for the troubled G-SIB.

However, in the acute phase, avoid time-consuming formal solvency tests that could delay support and create stigma. Use existing supervisory information, exclude institutions already determined nonviable, and coordinate separately on resolution or capital tools for insolvent firms.

### Collateral

Use a staged collateral framework:
1. **Stage 1:** Standing-facility collateral.
2. **Stage 2:** Add high-quality but less-liquid securities.
3. **Stage 3:** Add performing loan portfolios or own-name securitizations with conservative haircuts, if needed.

Collateral should be “sound before the panic,” not necessarily liquid during the panic. Apply haircuts, concentration limits, and extra margins for hard-to-value or foreign-currency collateral.

### Pricing

Avoid the mistake of pricing the program so punitively that no one uses it. Options:
- **Auction pricing:** Best for stigma reduction and price discovery.
- **Fixed-rate full allotment:** Best for severe systemic stress.
- **Penalty spread:** Should be modest in the acute phase; too high a spread can signal weakness and deter use.

A practical starting point: minimum auction rate modestly above the policy rate or OIS rate, but below stressed term bank funding rates.

### Maturity

Term funding is critical in a run. Offer:
- 28-day funds immediately;
- 84-day or 3-month funds as standard;
- 6- or 12-month terms if wholesale markets remain impaired.

Longer maturities reduce rollover risk but increase central bank credit exposure, so collateral controls must tighten with maturity.

### Size

Do not under-size the facility. A small program can backfire by suggesting the central bank misunderstands the scale of the run.

Options:
- Announce large fixed auction sizes and scale up if oversubscribed.
- Announce “as much as needed against eligible collateral” if panic is acute.
- Publish that the central bank is prepared to expand frequency, maturity, and allotments.

### Disclosure and stigma

During the acute phase:
- disclose aggregate use only;
- publish auction statistics without naming borrowers;
- delay borrower-level disclosure until after conditions normalize, if legally required.

Stigma is not cosmetic—it can determine whether the facility works. Historical experience shows that identifying emergency borrowers during a crisis can accelerate deposit withdrawals and market pressure.

### Communication

The announcement should be clear, confident, and operationally specific. It should say:
- the banking system has access to ample liquidity;
- the facility is broad-based and available to all eligible banks;
- the central bank will lend against sound collateral;
- the facility supplements standing arrangements;
- individual borrower use will not be disclosed during the acute phase;
- the central bank is prepared to adjust size, maturity, and collateral as needed.

Avoid communications that suggest the program exists only because of one failing institution.

## Complementary measures to consider

A lending facility can stop a liquidity run, but it cannot solve insolvency. Therefore:

1. **Supervisory/resolution plan for the G-SIB**
   - Separate the broad facility from any firm-specific intervention.
   - If the G-SIB is insolvent or nonviable, liquidity alone is insufficient.

2. **Deposit or liability guarantee**
   - If retail or wholesale creditors are running systemwide, lending may need to be paired with temporary guarantees.
   - Guarantees require fiscal authority involvement.

3. **Capital backstop**
   - If solvency concerns are widespread, prepare recapitalization or resolution tools.

4. **Foreign currency liquidity**
   - If the G-SIB or domestic banks face foreign-currency funding pressure, arrange swap lines or foreign-currency lending operations.

5. **Market-functioning operations**
   - If asset fire sales are impairing collateral markets, consider repo operations or asset-purchase facilities targeted at market liquidity.

## Recommended decision path

### Today
Announce a **Broad-Based Term Liquidity Facility**:
- weekly 28-day and 84-day auctions;
- all eligible supervised banks;
- broad standing-facility collateral;
- large initial allotments;
- aggregate-only disclosure.

At the same time, temporarily enhance the standing facility:
- longer maturities;
- lower penalty spread;
- expanded collateral;
- clear operational instructions.

### Within 48 hours
Prepare escalation:
- fixed-rate full-allotment mode;
- expanded collateral schedule;
- additional 3-month and 6-month operations;
- coordination with fiscal authority on guarantees if runs continue.

### If contagion accelerates
Switch to or add:
- **fixed-rate full-allotment term funding**;
- broader collateral;
- potentially temporary liability guarantees.

### Exit strategy
Design exit from the start:
- price facility above normal market funding so use declines naturally;
- reduce auction size and frequency as markets normalize;
- restore standard collateral and haircuts gradually;
- retain useful features as a standing contingency facility if appropriate.
