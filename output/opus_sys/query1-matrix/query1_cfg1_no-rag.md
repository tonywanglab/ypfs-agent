# Assessment Framework for Insurance Company Crisis

This is a serious situation that requires careful analysis before any commitment. Here's how I'd approach it.

## First, Establish the Facts

Before recommending anything, I need answers to critical questions:

- **Solvency vs. liquidity:** Is this a temporary liquidity squeeze (assets exist but can't be quickly converted to cash) or genuine insolvency (liabilities exceed assets)? These require very different responses.
- **Systemic importance:** How interconnected is the firm? "Globally significant" suggests real systemic risk, but I need specifics—counterparty exposures, policyholder counts, links to banks/pension funds.
- **Root cause:** Was this purely the disaster, or does it expose underlying mismanagement, under-reserving, or excessive risk-taking?

## Guiding Principles

1. **Protect policyholders and financial stability—not shareholders or management.** A bailout that rescues those who took the risks creates moral hazard.
2. **Minimize taxpayer exposure and cost.**
3. **Preserve market discipline.**

## Range of Options (Least to Most Interventionist)

- **Private-sector solution first:** Facilitate a capital raise, acquisition by a stronger competitor, or lending from private consortiums. The government's role may just be coordination.
- **Central bank / regulator liquidity support** (if truly a liquidity problem): Emergency lending against good collateral, at a penalty rate—so the firm has incentive to exit support quickly. This follows the classic "lender of last resort" logic.
- **Resolution / orderly wind-down:** If insolvent, use special resolution powers to protect policyholders (via guarantee schemes) while imposing losses on shareholders and possibly creditors.
- **Direct capital injection / nationalization:** Only as a last resort where systemic collapse is otherwise unavoidable—and with strict conditions.

## My Recommendation

**If liquidity-only and systemically important:** Provide *temporary, collateralized, penalty-priced* liquidity support—conditional on management changes, restrictions on dividends/bonuses, and a credible recovery plan. This stabilizes the system without a giveaway.

**If insolvent:** Do not bail out the entity. Use resolution tools to protect policyholders and ensure continuity of critical functions, while wiping out shareholders and holding management accountable.

**In all cases:** Attach strict conditions, demand transparency, and launch a review of why the firm was so exposed (regulatory gaps, catastrophe reinsurance failures, etc.).

## Key Caveats

- Beware moral hazard—rescuing this firm sets expectations for the next crisis.
- Avoid rushed decisions driven by market panic; announcing that authorities are actively managing the situation can itself calm markets.
- Coordinate internationally given the firm's global significance.
