# Summary of core design dimensions

Your supervisor can frame this as a **broad-based emergency liquidity backstop** for the acute phase of a systemic run: fast, simple, available to a wide class of banks, and designed to reassure depositors and short-term creditors that banks can meet outflows without fire sales. It should **supplement, not replace**, standing facilities and any institution-specific measures for the globally significant bank.

Key design choices:

1. **Purpose and trigger**
   - Purpose: arrest deposit/funding runs, prevent forced asset sales, and support functioning of payments and interbank markets.
   - Trigger: “actual or prospective market-wide liquidity stress,” ideally broad enough to allow preemptive activation.

2. **Eligible counterparties**
   - All domestic banks meeting prudential/supervisory access standards and operational readiness.
   - Consider including foreign bank branches/subsidiaries if they are material deposit-takers or payment-system participants.
   - Avoid narrow eligibility that signals which banks are distressed.

3. **Collateral**
   - Start with standing-facility collateral, then add high-quality but less-liquid securities and loan pools.
   - Require pre-positioning where feasible, but allow expedited review for emergency onboarding.
   - Use conservative haircuts, but avoid terms so punitive that the facility is unused.

4. **Maturity**
   - Overnight standing facilities are not enough in a run. Offer term funding: 1 month and 3 months immediately; consider 6 months if the aim is to bridge regulatory liquidity stress or prevent repeated rollover stigma.
   - Shorter maturities preserve control; longer maturities give banks more confidence and reduce rollover risk.

5. **Pricing**
   - A modest premium over the policy rate is appropriate, but too high a penalty can worsen stigma and non-use.
   - In severe systemic stress, fixed-rate full-allotment pricing is often preferable to scarce auctions.

6. **Size and allotment**
   - If the objective is confidence, either announce **no aggregate cap** or a very large envelope.
   - Use per-counterparty limits only if needed for risk control; overly tight limits can undermine credibility.

7. **Stigma and disclosure**
   - Publish aggregate usage, not bank-level usage during the crisis.
   - Use auctions or broad full-allotment operations so borrowing looks like systemwide liquidity management rather than a distress signal.
   - Communicate that use is prudent liquidity management, not a sign of weakness.

8. **Central bank balance-sheet protection**
   - Collateral, haircuts, margining, supervisory eligibility, and possibly fiscal indemnity for nonstandard collateral.
   - If the facility must accept unusually risky assets or lend on unusually favorable terms, seek explicit fiscal risk-sharing.

9. **Monetary policy interaction**
   - The facility will expand reserves. Decide whether to sterilize, allow reserve expansion, or offset through other operations.
   - Index the rate to the policy rate to avoid undermining the monetary policy stance.

10. **Exit**
   - Announce that the facility is temporary or contingent, but with authority to extend.
   - Taper by shortening maturities, raising spreads, narrowing collateral, or reducing frequency only after run pressure has clearly abated.

# Program Archetypes

## Option 1: Fixed-rate full-allotment emergency term repo facility

**Basic design**

- **Instrument:** collateralized term loans or repos.
- **Eligible banks:** all banks with standing-facility access, plus expedited onboarding for other supervised banks.
- **Collateral:** the widest collateral already acceptable in the central bank’s framework; add high-quality private securities, covered bonds, securitizations, and performing loan pools if operationally feasible.
- **Maturity:** 1 month and 3 months immediately; consider 6 months if liquidity stress is persistent.
- **Rate:** policy rate plus a modest spread, e.g., 10–50 bps depending on severity.
- **Allotment:** full allotment against eligible collateral, no aggregate cap.
- **Disclosure:** aggregate only, with delayed counterparty disclosure if legally required.

**Why this is likely the strongest immediate option**

This is the most direct confidence tool. The Bank of England’s contingent term repo experience is a close precedent: the facility was designed to provide liquidity against the widest collateral during actual or prospective market-wide stress, and in its 2020 activation it moved to fixed-price, full-allotment, unlimited-size operations at a modest spread over Bank Rate ([vol4_iss2_51][United Kingdom: Extended Collateral Term Repo Facility][case_study][2022-07-18T21:02:43Z]).

**Advantages**

- Fast to communicate.
- Reduces rollover anxiety.
- Minimizes stigma if broad access and full allotment are emphasized.
- Allows banks to meet outflows without fire-sale liquidation.
- Clear supplement to standing facilities because it adds term and/or collateral breadth.

**Risks**

- Increases central bank balance-sheet exposure.
- If pricing is too generous, may displace private funding.
- If collateral is too broad and haircuts too light, risk may shift to the central bank.
- If the troubled globally significant bank is fundamentally insolvent, this only buys time; it does not solve solvency.

**Best use case**

Use this if the central bank wants the strongest systemwide confidence signal within hours.

## Option 2: Emergency term auction facility

**Basic design**

- **Instrument:** term lending by auction.
- **Eligible banks:** broad set of supervised banks/payment-system participants.
- **Collateral:** standing-facility collateral plus selected less-liquid assets.
- **Maturity:** 1 month initially; 3 months if stress persists.
- **Pricing:** single-price auction with a minimum bid spread over the policy rate.
- **Size:** announce a large regular auction calendar, with authority to scale up.
- **Disclosure:** aggregate auction results only.

**Why consider it**

A term auction can reduce stigma because many banks can participate simultaneously and the market sees aggregate liquidity provision rather than institution-specific borrowing. Canada’s Term Loan Facility used weekly single-price auctions to provide one-month funding to major payment-system banks against non-mortgage loan portfolios; the facility was designed as a broad backstop and as a less-stigmatized alternative to emergency lending assistance ([vol4_iss2_35][Canada: Term Loan Facility][case_study][2022-07-18T21:02:33Z]).

**Advantages**

- Market-based allocation.
- Can discover demand and pricing.
- Useful where policymakers want some rationing and price discipline.
- Helps reduce stigma relative to bilateral emergency lending.

**Risks**

- Auctions can fail if stigma or pricing is too high.
- A fixed auction size may be too small in a run.
- Weekly cadence may be too slow in a fast-moving deposit run.
- If banks fear losing an auction, it may not reassure depositors.

**Best use case**

Use as a second-stage facility once the immediate panic has been stabilized, or pair it with a full-allotment facility as a regular liquidity channel.

## Option 3: Enhanced standing-facility overlay

**Basic design**

Temporarily modify existing standing facilities rather than creating a wholly new program:

- Extend maturities from overnight to 1–3 months.
- Broaden eligible collateral.
- Reduce or cap penalty spreads.
- Permit larger drawings.
- Expand eligible counterparties.
- Add a discretionary term lending window for collateral not easily processed through the standard facility.

Hong Kong’s 2008 temporary liquidity measures are a useful precedent. After a deposit run at Bank of East Asia and interbank-market tightening, the HKMA expanded discount-window terms, accepted additional collateral, lowered borrowing costs, and created discretionary term lending and foreign-exchange swap facilities for licensed banks ([vol4_iss2_41][Hong Kong: Temporary Liquidity Measures, 2008][case_study][2022-07-18T21:02:37Z]).

**Advantages**

- Fast if legal authority and operational plumbing already exist.
- Easier for banks to understand.
- Avoids creating a new legal vehicle.
- Can be announced as a temporary enhancement to normal liquidity insurance.

**Risks**

- May not overcome standing-facility stigma.
- If the standing window is already associated with distress, usage may remain low.
- Existing collateral systems may constrain scale.
- If access is bilateral and discretionary, markets may infer which banks are weak.

**Best use case**

Use if legal or operational constraints make a new program difficult, or as an immediate bridge while a broader facility is built.

## Option 4: Indexed / variable-supply term repo facility

**Basic design**

- Regular term repo auctions against multiple collateral pools.
- The facility automatically supplies more liquidity, and more liquidity against wider collateral, when bids reveal market stress.
- Uniform pricing within each collateral category.
- Eligible collateral divided into quality buckets with different minimum spreads and haircuts.
- Maturity: ideally 3–6 months.

The Bank of England’s Indexed Long-Term Repo operations are the leading design precedent. The ILTR used a multi-good auction so banks could bid against different collateral pools; later revisions made supply variable so the facility could automatically provide more liquidity against less-liquid collateral in stress ([vol4_iss2_52][United Kingdom: Indexed Long-Term Repo Operations][case_study][2022-07-18T21:02:43Z]).

**Advantages**

- Sophisticated, rules-based, and less ad hoc.
- Can provide early-warning information through bids and spreads.
- Supports market discipline while adapting to stress.
- Good permanent addition to the toolkit.

**Risks**

- Too complex to launch from scratch in the middle of a run.
- Banks need to understand bidding mechanics.
- Operationally demanding.
- Less suitable as the primary “today/tomorrow” confidence measure.

**Best use case**

Use as a medium-term stabilization tool or as the successor to a simpler emergency full-allotment facility.

## Option 5: Targeted collateral-relief term funding facility

**Basic design**

This option is useful if the run is linked to unrealized losses or illiquidity in otherwise high-quality securities.

- **Instrument:** term advances against high-quality government, agency, covered bond, or other eligible securities.
- **Valuation:** lend against amortized cost/par value for a narrow set of government-guaranteed assets, or use market value with reduced haircuts for broader assets.
- **Maturity:** 6 months to 1 year.
- **Rate:** policy rate plus a modest spread.
- **Eligibility:** all banks with eligible assets.

**Advantages**

- Directly addresses the “good assets, bad market price” problem.
- Reduces forced sales of sovereign or agency securities.
- Strong confidence signal if depositors fear mark-to-market losses.

**Risks**

- Valuing collateral above market shifts interest-rate and credit risk to the public sector.
- May require fiscal indemnity.
- Can appear to subsidize banks that mismanaged interest-rate risk.
- Less useful if the run is driven by credit losses or solvency doubts.

**Best use case**

Use if the contagion channel is forced sales or mark-to-market losses on high-quality securities, rather than credit impairment.

# Complementary Considerations

1. **Do not rely on liquidity alone for the globally significant bank**
   - A broad facility can stop contagion, but if the lead institution is insolvent or nonviable, it needs a parallel plan: resolution, merger, capital injection, liability guarantee, or other structural intervention.
   - The broad facility should be communicated as a systemwide backstop, not as the rescue plan for the failing bank.

2. **Pair with deposit and liability measures if the run is depositor-led**
   - If depositors are running because they doubt access to funds, liquidity may be insufficient without deposit-insurance clarification, temporary guarantee expansion, or public assurance.
   - Hong Kong combined liquidity measures with broader confidence measures including a full deposit guarantee and a contingent bank capital facility ([vol4_iss2_41][Hong Kong: Temporary Liquidity Measures, 2008][case_study][2022-07-18T21:02:37Z]).

3. **Reduce stigma explicitly**
   - Announce that supervisors expect prudent banks to use the facility.
   - Encourage broad early participation by large healthy banks.
   - Avoid counterparty-level disclosure during the acute phase.
   - Keep pricing above normal market rates in calm times, but not so high that only desperate banks use it.

4. **Collateral readiness is critical**
   - Ask banks immediately to pre-position collateral.
   - Create fast-track collateral review teams.
   - Standardize haircuts and legal documentation.
   - Consider fiscal indemnity for loan pools or nonstandard collateral.

5. **Foreign currency liquidity**
   - If domestic banks have material USD, EUR, GBP, CHF, or JPY funding needs, activate swap-line-based facilities or foreign-currency repo operations.
   - A domestic-currency facility will not stop a foreign-currency run.

6. **Communication package**
   - Announce the facility with:
     - purpose;
     - eligible institutions;
     - collateral;
     - maturity;
     - pricing;
     - size/allotment;
     - disclosure policy;
     - expected duration;
     - statement that the banking system has access to ample liquidity.
   - Avoid implying that all banks are weak; emphasize that the facility prevents market dysfunction and fire sales.

7. **Exit strategy**
   - Do not announce an end date that is too near. Use “available as long as conditions warrant.”
   - Exit sequence:
     1. reduce frequency;
     2. shorten maturities;
     3. raise spreads;
     4. narrow collateral;
     5. close new drawings while allowing existing loans to mature.

# Suggested Framing

I would present three recommended choices to your supervisor:

**Preferred immediate package:**  
Launch a **fixed-rate, full-allotment emergency term repo facility** available to all eligible banks, offering 1- and 3-month funding against the broadest eligible collateral, with aggregate-only disclosure. This is the clearest confidence backstop and has strong precedent in the Bank of England’s contingent term repo approach ([vol4_iss2_51][United Kingdom: Extended Collateral Term Repo Facility][case_study][2022-07-18T21:02:43Z]).

**Backup / supplement:**  
Add a **regular term auction facility** once the acute phase stabilizes, especially if the central bank wants market-based pricing and a scheduled liquidity channel. Canada’s Term Loan Facility shows the usefulness of a term auction backstop, but also the risk that penalty pricing and narrower design can limit usage ([vol4_iss2_35][Canada: Term Loan Facility][case_study][2022-07-18T21:02:33Z]).

**Medium-term toolkit improvement:**  
Develop a more durable **indexed or variable-supply term repo facility** that automatically expands liquidity against wider collateral as stress rises, drawing on the Bank of England’s ILTR design ([vol4_iss2_52][United Kingdom: Indexed Long-Term Repo Operations][case_study][2022-07-18T21:02:43Z]).

The key message: **lend broadly, early, and against a wide but risk-managed collateral set; keep pricing usable; protect anonymity; and handle any insolvent institution through separate resolution or fiscal tools.**
